Splash Weapons
![[Pasted image 20251013101629.png]]

A splash weapon is a ranged weapon that breaks on impact, splashing or scattering its contents over its target and nearby creatures or objects. To attack with a splash weapon, make a ranged touch attack against the target. Splash weapons require no weapon proficiency, so you don’t take the -4 non-proficiency penalty. A hit deals direct hit damage to the target, and splash damage to all creatures within 5 feet of the target.

- You can instead target a specific grid intersection. Treat this as a ranged attack against AC 5. However, if you target a grid intersection, creatures in all adjacent squares are dealt the splash damage, and the direct hit damage is not dealt to any creature. (You can’t target a grid intersection occupied by a creature, such as a Large or larger creature; in this case, you’re aiming at the creature.)
- If you miss the target (whether aiming at a creature or a grid intersection), roll 1d8. This determines the misdirection of the throw, with 1 being straight back at you and 2 through 8 counting clockwise around the grid intersection or target creature. Then, count a number of squares in the indicated direction equal to the range increment of the throw.
- After you determine where the weapon landed, it deals splash damage to all creatures in adjacent squares.
- Some splash weapons may be arcanotech devices; as such they do not function in antimagic zones.
- If improperly stored, splash weapons may all detonate simultaneously upon death or upon being critically injured.

Making Splash Weapons

Most splash weapons are alchemical or arcanotech in nature, and as such their creation requires special attention. Arcanotech grenades and bombs can be made in a Gun Lab (Either Basic or Advanced), while regular alchemical splash weapons can be made in an Alchemist's Lab. Arcanotech splash weapons utilize elemental cores, but once created they are inert until triggered. Elemental cores can influence the type of damage such a weapon deals. The Craft DC for such a weapon is generally equal to 15 + 1 per core. A character cannot craft an arcanotech splash weapon with more cores than 1 + their Intelligence modifier; should they attempt to the weapon will promptly explode immediately upon completion. As a general rule, cores are very volatile, and should be carried in capsulum.

Additional Components

Additional components may be added to a splash weapon to add more utility to them. Such components include adhesive gel which allows for the weapon to stick to a surface or target, and a timer to delay the detonation of the splash weapon. To install such a component, see Improvised Payloads below.

Improvised Payloads

Splash weapons are typically designed to trigger on contact, and can be rigged together to detonate simultaneously for a bigger payload. Combining splash weapons of the same type (such as multiple flasks of acid) can be done relatively easily with an appropriate Craft check (DC 10 + 1 per component). Combining similar splash weapons (of the same category, such as flasks of acid with flasks of alchemist fire) can also be done with an appropriate Craft check but the DC is higher (DC 11 + 2 per component). Combining dissimilar splash weapons (such as alchemical splash weapons with mundane splash weapons) is much harder to do, requiring a DC 15 + 3 per component Craft check. Alternatively, additional components may be added to the improvised payload, allowing for further customization. Such a component is treated as a dissimilar component. Failing the Craft check by less than 5 results in the payload (and all components) becoming inert; failing the Craft check by 5 or more results in the payload immediately detonating in your hands. An improvised payload cannot be composed of more than 5 components, otherwise the creation automatically fails and detonates. Crafting an improvised payload takes at least one full minute (10 consecutive full-round actions), but it can be rushed as a full round action at a -10 penalty to the check.

An improvised payload does not necessarily deal the same damage as its components, instead the following rules apply:

- Every additional two components of the same type doubles the base damage; IE an improvised payload of three flasks of acid would deal 2d6 points of acid damage while two flasks would still only deal 1d6 points of acid damage. This applies to splash damage as well, if applicable.
- Additional components of similar types combine damage types and increases base damage by one stage. The same damage type combination rule applies still; IE an improvised load composed of a flask of acid and flask of alchemist's fire deals 1d8 points of damage and it is considered both fire and acid. An improvised load composed of three flasks of acid and a flask of alchemist's fire would deal 2d8 points of fire/acid damage.
- Additional components of dissimilar types simply append the appropriate damage or effects to the payload; IE an improvised payload of holy water and a flask of acid would deal 1d6 points of acid damage and 2d4 points of sacred damage (if applicable).
