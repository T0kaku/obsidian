### Leadership Feat

- The _Leadership_ feat is not allowed.
    - It drags out gameplay, is unbalanced, and can easily grant 50+ followers plus a companion nearly as strong as the your own character.
        
    - Spells and abilities with similar effects are generally still allowed.
        

### Flying in Combat

- Using flight purely to become untouchable in combat is not allowed.
    
- Flying is fine for flavor, exploration, or repositioning.
    
- If a player strongly wants to use flight in combat, we can discuss balanced options.
    

### Changelings

- High-ranking NPCs will often see through disguises/ have people that can see trough disguises.
    
- You can’t, for example, walk into the royal treasury disguised as the king to steal epic-level magic items.

### Attacks of Opportunity
   Opportunity attacks are removed when it comes to movement (except when moving directly through an opponent’s space). The standard rule makes retreat nearly impossible and discourages tactical repositioning, which often results in both players and monsters standing still, trading attacks back and forth without using the battlefield. This creates repetitive “staring match” combat where terrain, flanking, and mobility are ignored. By removing movement-based opportunity attacks, combat becomes more dynamic and strategic, allowing both sides to make use of the environment, reposition for advantage, and engage with abilities that rely on movement. Monsters will also move more actively, creating a sense of flow and danger instead of everyone forming a static circle of attacks.

To keep fights interesting, I’ll use mechanics that encourage movement, inspired by MMO-style mechanics and battlefield control. These will usually include a clear warning or “tell” before the effect resolves. Think of them like **Battlefield Actions** (similar to Legendary Actions), happening at the end of the monster’s own turn.

**Examples:**

1. **Summoning Ritual**
    
    - The boss begins chanting. Three magic circles appear, energy forming into monster shapes.
        
    - Standing on the circles prevents the summons from completing.
        
    - Spells like _Silence_ could also disrupt the casting.
        
2. **Energy Blast Retaliation**
    
    - The boss forms an energy orb aimed at the last player who attacked it.
        
    - If another player attacks before the orb fires, the target changes.
        
    - This can’t be canceled, but players can strategize to ensure the damage goes to someone who can endure it.
        
    - Positioning matters — standing too close together risks hitting multiple allies.

Here’s a breakdown of the main differences you’d need to cover, organized so your 5e players can quickly adjust:

---

## 📜 Core Differences Between 5e and 3.5

### 1. Ability Scores & Modifiers

- **Same ability scores** (STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA).
    
- Modifier is still `(score - 10) / 2, rounded down`.
    
- **Point buy / rolling** works differently — clarify which method you’re using.
    

---

### 2. Races & Classes

- Many options overlap, but some races/classes/subclasses are different or missing.
    
- **Prestige Classes** exist (like advanced subclasses you qualify for later).
    
- Multiclassing requires **meeting prerequisites** (not automatic like 5e).
    

---

### 3. Leveling & XP

- XP tables are **different** (3.5 has steeper progression).
    
- Level-up includes **skill points and feats** much more often.
    

---

### 4. Skills

- Huge difference from 5e!
    
- 3.5 uses **skill ranks**:
    
    - Each level you get skill points based on class + INT modifier.
        
    - You buy **ranks in individual skills** (e.g., Hide, Move Silently, Listen instead of Stealth/Perception).
        
    - Class skills cost **1 point per rank**, cross-class cost **2 points per rank** and have a lower max cap.
        
- Skills are rolled as: `d20 + ranks + ability mod + misc`.
    

---

### 5. Feats

- Much more important than in 5e.
    
- Characters get a **feat at 1st level**, then every **3rd level** (3, 6, 9, etc.), plus extra from class/race.
    
- Many feats are required to use combat maneuvers (like Power Attack, Two-Weapon Fighting, etc.).
    

---

### 6. Saving Throws

- 3 categories: **Fortitude, Reflex, Will** (instead of 5e’s 6 saves).
    
- Each class gives you a progression (good or bad) in these saves.
    

---

### 7. Combat Rules

- **BAB (Base Attack Bonus)** instead of proficiency bonus.
    
    - Higher-level fighters get **multiple attacks per round** (e.g., +12/+7/+2).
        
- **Armor Class** works the same (but touch AC and flat-footed AC exist).
    
- **Attack of Opportunity** rules are stricter and more common.
    
- **Flanking** gives +2 to attack rolls (not advantage).
    
- **Grapple, Trip, Bull Rush, etc.** all have specific mechanics with opposed rolls.
    
- Ranged weapons provoke Attacks of Opportunity if fired in melee.
    

---

### 8. Movement & Actions

- **Action types**: Standard, Move, Swift, Full-Round, Free.
    
- Casting many spells = **full-round actions**.
    
- Drawing a weapon usually = **move action** unless you have a feat.
    

---

### 9. Spellcasting

- Vancian “spell slots” but prepared differently than 5e:
    
    - **Wizards** prepare specific spells into slots.
        
    - **Clerics/Druids** prepare from their whole list but must assign each slot.
        
    - **Sorcerers** know a small number of spells and cast spontaneously.
        
- **No cantrips at-will** — even 0-level spells have limited slots.
    
- **Concentration checks** to cast defensively (if threatened in melee).
    

---

### 10. Magic Items

- Much more **expected at low levels** than in 5e.
    
- Wealth by Level guidelines assume PCs will be loaded with gear.
    
- Crafting is common if you take the right feats.
    

---

### 11. Death & Dying

- At 0 HP = **disabled**, at –1 to –9 HP = **dying**, at –10 HP = **dead**.
    
- No “death saves.”
    

---

### 12. Rest & Healing

- Natural healing is very slow compared to 5e.
    
- Magic healing (Cure Light Wounds, wands, etc.) is basically required.
    

---

### 13. Alignment

- Strict alignment rules matter (especially for paladins, clerics, and certain spells).
    
- More mechanical consequences (Detect Evil, alignment restrictions).
    

---

## ✅ Quick Cheat Sheet for 5e Players

- Forget proficiency bonus — use **BAB** instead.
    
- Your **skills list is longer**, and you must spend points each level.
    
- You’ll get **lots of feats** — but you need them to fight effectively.
    
- Remember **three saves (Fort, Ref, Will)**, not six.
    
- **Magic is harder**: no free cantrips, you prepare differently, and you can fail when casting under pressure.
    
- **Combat is crunchier**: expect more rolls for grapples, AoOs, multiple attacks, etc.
    
- Don’t forget **touch AC** and **flat-footed AC**.
    
- **Healing is slower** — magic items are assumed to keep you alive.

The action economy in 5E is very simple. You have one Action and one Bonus Action per turn and one Reaction per round. You can move up to your speed during your turn but this can be before your action, after your action or split so partially before and partially after.

Pretty much everything you would do is an Action, including taking advantage of multiple attacks built into the Attack action for dual weilding or the martial extra attack features. There is only one standard Reaction, which is the Opportunity Attack and Bonus Actions can only be granted by spells and class features.


**general**





**Character Creation**

- Proficiency Bonus is base modifier for attack rolls, checks, saves that you are proficient with (determined by class). Class features (spell save DCs, etc) also drive off it. Start at +2 for Level 1 and goes up 1 every 4 levels. (p. 7)
    
- Classes have archetypes/traditions/domains to specialize their abilities and features
    
- XP progression (p. 10)
    
- Inspiration (either you have it or you don't, granted by DM for playing to your characters flaws and traits) can be spent to gain advantage for a roll, cancel a disadvantage, or given to another player. You may only have one at a time. (p. 35)
    

**Races**

- Racial bonus traits are very different. Ability bonus (no penalties in the Basic races). Humans get +1 to each ability.
    

**Classes**

- A Rogue's Uncanny Dodge halves damage but uses Reaction (p. 28)
    

**Spellcasting**

- Spell DCs are based on "8 + Ability + Proficiency" instead of "10 + Ability + Level of Spell" (p. 81)
    
- Clerics and wizards prepare subset of known spells, then cast from the prepared list like sorcerers (p. 78)
    
- 0 level spells (aka, cantrips) are not exhausted (p. 78)
    
- Spell ranges/durations/effects are much less mathy (most are fixed numbers instead of per-level calculations)
    

**Using Ability Scores**

- Ability increases schedule determined by class, cap at 20 (p. 57)
    
- Advantage/Disadvantage: Roll 2d20 take highest/lowest (p. 57)
    
- No Fort/Dex/Will saves, each ability can make its own save, with possible proficiency bonus based on class (p. 62)
    
- Working Together: If two or more characters attempt a skill check together (they both must be able to attempt it), the character leading the effort gets to check with advantage.
    

**Combat**

- 20 is a critical hit, no confirmation or weapon-specific ranges/multipliers. Doubles dice, single modifiers. (p. 73 and 75)
    
- Opportunity attacks are provoked when moving out of a foe's reach, avoided by Disengage (standard action). Spend Reaction (you have one reaction per turn) to make attack. (p. 74)
    
- Instead of provoking an attack when performing a ranged attack (weapon, spell, or other) when adjacent (within 5') to a foe, you make the attack at disadvantage (p. 73)
    
- Grappling (p. 74)
    
- Cover: Half, three-quarters, full (p. 74)
    
- Instant Death: Damage past 0 that equals your max hp (p. 76)
    
- Death Saving Throws: 10+ (no modifiers) to pass, need 3 success to become stable, 3 failures to die. 20 gets you 1 hp, 1 gets you 2 failures. (p. 76)
    
- Knock out: Melee attack that takes creature to 0 hp can be a knock out at the player's choice (p. 76)
    
- Bonus Action (if you have something that grants you one) works like Swift Action (p. 69)
    
- Actions/attacks can be interspersed with movement, turn is one action and movement (p. 70)
    
- Damage reduction, resistances, vulnerabilities just halve or double damage taken (p. 75)
    
- Two-Weapon Fighting: You can attack twice if you are wielding 2 light weapons. First attack is normal, second doesn't use ability mod to the damage unless it's negative. You can also throw 2 light weapons if they have the thrown property. (p. 74)
    
- Grid more optional (p. 71)
    

Things that are "gone":

- Feats are optional rule, can be "bought" with Ability increases
    
- Skills don't have points assigned, you are either proficient or not
    
- No 5' step.
    
- No full-round attacks (but classes that have multiple attacks can intersperse them while moving)
    
- Flanking (but sneak attack damage can be granted from adjacent ally or Advantage)

There's a lot, probably too many to list, but it's helpful to just read them to see if they "clicked". Here's a couple that stood out to me as someone who hasn't been following the playtest:

- "You can make an opportunity attack when a hostile creature that you can see moves out of your reach." So that means you can dance around a baddie without provoking attack of opportunities, right?
    
- Uncanny Dodge: "When an attacker that you can see hits you with an attack, you can use your reaction to halve the attack's damage against you" That's such a departure from 3.5 I wish they had used a different name.
    
- Working Together: If two or more characters attempt a skill check together (they both must be able to attempt it), the character leading the effort gets to check with advantage. As opposed to 3.5's 10DC to add +2 to roll.





And a few more:

- No flanking in the basic rules. However, a rogue can still get sneak attack damage if an ally is also next to the target or the rogue has advantage.
    
- Attacks Of Opportunities (3.5) vs. Opportunity Attacks: Much less things provoke them in 5e.No OA for Standing Up, Casting Spells (no more combat casting!), or Ranged Attacks. As far as I can tell, in 5e only moving out of reach of an opponent provokes.
    
- Spell DCs are based on "8 + Ability + Proficiency" instead of "10 + Ability + Level of Spell".
    
- Finesse Attribute on some weapons: Allows character to use Dex for both damage and attack bonus. Removes some of the feat tax that plagued 3.5.

Remember these are just from the starter set, there will be additional rules once the PHB is out that could trump these:

Crit is auto hit no matter the AC and roll damage dice twice, add modifiers once. Fumble is auto miss, no other rules.

Two-Weapon Fighting - You can attack twice if you are wielding 2 light weapons. First attack is normal, second doesn't add ability mods to the damage unless it's negative. You can also throw 2 light weapons if they have the thrown property.

Disengage is a standard action, but can still use your move and not provoke any attacks of opportunity. No 5' step rule.





Damage Reduction and resistance to types of damage have all been simplified down to resistance/vulnerability. Resistance to a type of damage (bludgeoning, fire, poison, etc) means that type of damage is halved, vulnerability means that it is doubled.

This is probably my favorite change from 3.x as it combines two very similar rules into one simple one. This may also be in forth, I don't know, I've only ever played 3.x.

SKILLS! Rather than using skill points classes get to chose a number of skills from a set list of "class skills" to which they can add their proficiency bonus.

This is a change that I like a bit less, but that's just cus building needlessly complicated skill monkeys in 3.x can be a ton of fun. I would have also have liked seeing every point of int bonus give more skills somehow, but that doesn't seem to exist.

Skills: Two come from class (At least, rogue gets four), two come from Background. Some classes have Expertise feature which doubles skill bonus for selected skills (Like the rogue that gets two skills for expertise at 1 and two more at 6).

Background: Gives your two of your skills, proficiency with some kind of tool, appropriate starting gear, and a role-play hook depending. Nobles are treated like nobles by common people, former mercenaries are respected by their old organization, sages know where to find information if they fail knowledge rolls, etc.

Conditions: Condition list is much smaller and much easier to remember.

Static DCs: All skill DCs come in 5 point increments, 10, 15, 20, 25 etc to a max of 35 (normally only attainable by gods and incredibly powerful outsiders).

Contests: Many more parts of the game are solved by opposed skill/ability rolls, allowing for more improvisation. Want to disarm a foe? Str/Dex vs Str/Dex (Attacker and Defender's Choice). The limit is imagination.

Bounded Accuracy: AC generally doesn't go over 25, attack bonus scales to 11 (without class features, spells, magic items, etc). Hit points and damage are the major scaling tools. A large group of weak enemies still poses a challenge to high-level players as it takes a lot to make them fish for twenties to do damage.
